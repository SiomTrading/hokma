SOURCES OF THE DATA FILES (SPEC-020, block B2: L-01, L-02, L-03, L-08)

All files are public exchange facts (contract specifications, performance bonds, trading calendars) written down as plain tables. Nothing
here is typed from memory: every value was transcribed by a script from the public data set named in .notices/THIRD_PARTY.txt (Apache-2.0
licensed, reused as data with the notice), and the tests check the values that matter against hand computed expectations and against the
exchange specification table of the contracts module (futures_specs.json).

futures_properties.csv
  One row per root: exchange, description, contract multiplier and minimum price variation (tick) in USD. Values as published by the
  exchanges; numbers are written without trailing zeros (50.0 is 50). The history uses them for any past date; in live trading the sheet
  of the provider decides (decision 1251) and contracts.history.differences compares the two.

futures_roots.csv
  One row per root: exchange, asset class, the listed contract months as month letters (cycle), what a month outside the cycle means
  (yes: no contract; forward: moves to the next listed month; no: the month lists) and the name of the expiration rule that
  contracts.expiry applies. The rules are the public contract specifications (last trading day of each product); the 355 vectors of
  backend/tests/contracts/fixtures/expiry_vectors.csv check them.

margins/<ROOT>.csv
  Exchange initial and maintenance margin per contract in USD, one row each time the exchange changed it (date is the first day in force).
  The series end in January 2022 for the equity index, metal and energy roots and earlier for some others; history.margin treats
  2022-01-21 as the end of the series for every root (SPEC-020 L-02). After it, the margin is the current sheet value and the result carries
  the warning historical_margin_missing. There is no data for 2022 to 2026: it is a gap, it is reported and it is never filled.

market_hours.json
  Weekly trading segments in the exchange time zone (premarket and postmarket are the extended session, market is the regular session),
  the dates the exchange is closed all day (the product's own list joined with the exchange's list), the early closes and the late opens
  of each date, and the bank holidays that the expiration rules count as non-business days. Roots with identical tables share one
  schedule. The equity index schedules (ES, MES, NQ, MNQ, RTY, M2K and YM, MYM) carry no bank holiday in October and November: the real
  daily sessions of NQ 12-25 in Trading Core (tests/backtest/fixtures/nq_session.json, 2025-09-15 to 2025-12-19) show full sessions on
  Columbus Day (2025-10-13) and Veterans Day (2025-11-11) and a session that ends at 13:00 New York time on Thanksgiving (2025-11-27, 46575
  contracts against about 500000 on a normal day), so those days are business days of the product (Thanksgiving keeps its early close and its
  evening reopening in the table). Only NQ is checked against real data; the other equity index roots share its schedule by family. The
  schedules of rates (ZN, ZB, ZF, ZT), energy (CL, MCL, NG), metals (GC, MGC, SI, HG) and currencies (6E) keep their bank holidays and
  early closes as transcribed: no real bars of those roots on those days exist in the repository, so they are unverified. contracts.sessions reads it: a day with only an early close ends at that time, a day with only a late open starts at that time,
  a day with both closes early and reopens late.
